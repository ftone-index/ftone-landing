What it is

A composite reading of monetary credibility, updated continuously.

The FCI answers one question: how credible is the U.S. monetary system right now? It draws on four independent signals — what the Fed communicates, what markets price, what the sovereign holds in hard assets, and how aligned those signals are — and folds them into a single 0–100 score. A higher score means higher credibility. A lower score means the system is losing the trust of at least one major constituency.

The four inputs

  • Fed Sentiment (FTN) — AI-scored Federal Reserve documents
  • Market Pricing — 2Y and 10Y Treasury yields, US Dollar Index
  • Gold Anchor — U.S. official gold stock vs. Fed liabilities and federal debt
  • Alignment — a diagnostic comparison of FTN and market pricing

Who it is for

  • Macro portfolio managers and CIOs
  • Central bank and sovereign wealth analysts
  • Journalists covering monetary policy
  • Any professional who needs a fast, transparent read on Fed credibility

FCI — Federal Reserve Credibility Index

The FCI is a composite 0–100 score measuring the market's real-time assessment of U.S. monetary credibility. 100 = highest credibility. 0 = severe credibility stress. The composite combines two layers: a Core layer capturing Fed stance and market pricing, and a Sovereign layer capturing hard-asset backing of the monetary and fiscal system.

Composite formula

FCI = 0.6 × Core + 0.4 × Sovereign

Weights

LayerComponentWeight
Core (60% of FCI) Fed Stance 30%
Market Pricing 30%
Sovereign (40% of FCI) Monetary Base Gold Cover 20%
Sovereign Debt Gold Cover 20%

Fed Stance sub-formula

Fed Stance = 0.7 × FTN + 0.3 × Policy Rate Calibration Policy Rate Calibration = 50 − 25 × (Real Policy Rate − Neutral Rate) Real Policy Rate = Fed Funds − Core PCE YoY (neutral rate = 0.5%)

Interpretation bands

  • 80–100 High credibility
  • 60–80 Moderate credibility
  • 40–60 Mixed credibility
  • 20–40 Low credibility
  • 0–20 Stressed

Fed Sentiment — the FTN component

The Fed Sentiment component measures the tone of Federal Reserve communication using AI analysis of official documents. It is the primary "what the Fed says" input to the credibility composite.

Sources

Every Fed communication is processed the moment it is published, from six categories:

  • FOMC statements Federal Reserve Board press releases
  • FOMC minutes Published three weeks after each meeting
  • Fed speeches Board governors and regional presidents
  • Regional Fed speeches Federal Reserve Bank of New York
  • Congressional testimony Fed Chair and governors
  • Liberty Street Economics Federal Reserve Bank of New York research

Scoring

Each document is scored by AI on a 0–100 scale. 0 = extremely dovish (signalling rate cuts or easing). 100 = extremely hawkish (signalling rate hikes or tightening). The score reflects two factors, weighed equally: imminence — how soon a change to the federal funds rate is expected — and sharpness — how large or decisive that change appears.

The FTN score is the smoothed 7-day average of daily document scores. The live score, shown alongside, reflects today's documents only.

Confidence indicator

Every reading carries a confidence label reflecting the breadth and volume of source material:

  • HIGH Four or more sources, more than 8,000 characters
  • MEDIUM Two or more sources, more than 3,000 characters
  • LOW Fewer sources or less text

Radical transparency

Every score is traceable to the exact source document that produced it. Every document is linked, timestamped, and attributed. No black boxes. No hidden logic.

Market Pricing

The Market Pricing component measures the direction of Fed policy priced by markets. It uses the same 0–100 scale as FTN — dovish to hawkish — but is derived from market data rather than documents. A hawkish Market Pricing reading means the market is pricing tighter-for-longer, independent of what the Fed documents say.

Inputs and weights

InputSourceWeight
2Y Treasury yieldFRED (DGS2)40%
10Y Treasury yieldFRED (DGS10)30%
US Dollar IndexYahoo Finance (DXY)30%

Scoring

Each input is converted to a 0–100 percentile score using a linear mapping to its reference range:

2Y: 2% → 0 8% → 100 10Y: 2% → 0 8% → 100 DXY: 90 → 0 120 → 100

Higher yields signal tighter expected policy and therefore a higher Market Pricing score. A stronger dollar signals tighter global dollar conditions and likewise pushes the score higher.

Interpretation bands

  • 80–100 Extremely hawkish
  • 60–80 Hawkish
  • 40–60 Neutral
  • 20–40 Dovish
  • 0–20 Extremely dovish

Gold Anchor

The Gold Anchor component measures the hard-asset backing of U.S. monetary and fiscal obligations. It is not a legal solvency metric. It is an analytical transparency measure showing how much of the system's outstanding liabilities would be covered by the sovereign's official gold stock, valued at market price.

Holdings

The U.S. official gold stock is approximately 261.5 million fine troy ounces (261,498,926 oz). It is owned by the U.S. Treasury and custodied by the Federal Reserve.

Source: U.S. Treasury, Status Report of U.S. Government Gold Reserve.

Two coverage ratios

Monetary Base Cover = gold market value ÷ Fed total liabilities Sovereign Debt Cover = gold market value ÷ Federal debt held by the public

The two ratios share the same gold numerator but different denominators. They are not additive — the same gold cannot be counted twice.

Why two

Monetary Base Cover answers: how much of the Fed's money-like liabilities (currency, reserves, RRP) would be covered by the sovereign's gold at today's market price? This is a monetary-system credibility metric.

Sovereign Debt Cover answers: how much of the sovereign debt owed to outside creditors would be covered by gold? This is a fiscal credibility metric.

Score

Gold Anchor = 0.5 × MB cover percentile + 0.5 × Sovereign cover percentile

Interpretation bands

  • 80–100 Strong backing
  • 60–80 Adequate backing
  • 40–60 Limited backing
  • 20–40 Weak backing
  • 0–20 Critical backing

Alignment — a diagnostic reading

Alignment measures the consistency between what the Federal Reserve communicates (FTN) and what markets price (Market Pricing).

Formula

Alignment = 100 − |FTN − Market Pricing|

If FTN = 64 and Market Pricing = 73, Alignment = 100 − 9 = 91.

Interpretation

High alignment (≥80) means markets and the Fed are on the same page — the credibility reading is more reliable.

Low alignment (<60) signals divergence — markets may be questioning Fed credibility.

Not an FCI input

Alignment is a diagnostic only. It does not enter the FCI composite formula. It is displayed alongside the composite so the user can assess the internal consistency of the reading.
